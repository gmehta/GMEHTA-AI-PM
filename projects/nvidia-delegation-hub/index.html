NVIDIA

Employee AI — the delegation generation

Product concept · Sr PM, Internal AI & Employee Productivity · Gaurav Mehta
NVInfo proved employees trust AI for answers. The next product earns the right to do the work: a single front door where a question becomes an answer, an answer becomes a plan, and a plan becomes a supervised delegation — on web and mobile. The unit of work shifts from a conversation to a delegation. That's how 36,000 employees start managing toward 100 agents each.
1 · The ideal employee day — five verbs, one surface
Ask

Cited answers from enterprise knowledge, scoped to your access. Trust starts here.

Triage

Your day arrives ranked — agents prep it overnight from email, Slack, docs, calendar.

Delegate

Hand off outcomes. Every answer offers a plan: "here's what I'd do — want me to?"

Supervise

15-second, full-context approvals from any device — desk, phone, lock screen.

Trust

Approvals become autonomy. Agents climb L0→L4 per user, per policy. The fleet grows.

3 · The generational shift this product makes

Today — assistants answer

  • Reactive: employee arrives with a question; AI responds
  • Unit of work: a conversation, resolved in-session
  • Fragmented: each team's bot lives in its own silo and UI
  • Desktop-first: value ends when you stand up from the desk

    • Next — agents deliver

  • Proactive: the day arrives pre-triaged, ranked like a feed
  • Unit of work: a delegation that runs async for hours or days
  • Federated: every team publishes agents to shared surfaces via one registry
  • Any device: agents keep working; supervision travels with you
    • 4 · Product brief

    The problem

    Employees don't lack information — NVInfo already answers questions across ~500M documents. They lack time: work signals are fragmented across email, Slack, docs, Jira and calendars, and acting on them is still 100% human effort. Meanwhile capable agents exist across NVIDIA — each in its own team's silo, with its own interface.

    The insight

    The bottleneck to 100 agents per employee isn't agent capability — it's trust and supervision cost. Employees delegate only what they can supervise cheaply. So the product is the supervision layer: one surface to ask, receive triaged work, hand it off, watch it run, and approve the moments that matter, from any device.

    For the uncertain employee: plan preview

    Nobody chooses between "chat" and "delegate." One command bar takes anything. A question returns an answer. An actionable ask returns the answer plus the agent's plan — what it would do, step by step, with its autonomy level — before anything runs. Take the answer and walk away, or run the plan. Certainty is never a prerequisite; nothing executes blind.

    Product principles

  • One command bar, zero modes. Intent is the system's job to classify, not the user's job to declare.
  • Role-aware by default, personal by learning. A developer's fleet ≠ a VP's: starter fleets, autonomy defaults and interruption budgets follow the role; ranking adapts to the person.
  • Delegating must beat doing. <10 seconds from intent to a running agent — internal tools get no marketing budget.
  • Trust is a UX deliverable. Citations on every answer; a plan before every action; a glass-box trace behind every delegation.
  • Autonomy is earned, not configured. Approval history promotes agents up the ladder per user, per policy.
  • Platform over product. Teams publish their own agents to a governed registry; this product owns the surfaces, rules and quality bar.
  • Customer zero. Built on NeMo, Nemotron and AI-Q; every interaction feeds the data flywheel NVIDIA ships to customers.

    • The autonomy ladder (L0–L4) — the spine of the product

    Bounded autonomy: each level trades supervision cost for risk. The product's job is moving agents up safely.

    LevelAgent may…Primary risk → mitigation
    L0 ReadRetrieve & summarize (RBAC-scoped)Hallucination → citations mandatory
    L1 SuggestDraft the action; human executesBad recommendations → one-tap edit/reject
    L2 ApproveExecute after explicit human OKApproval fatigue → 15-sec context-rich cards, batching, promotion offers
    L3 PolicyExecute within budget/policy guardrailsRunaway execution → spend caps, rollback, audit trail
    L4 AutoOwn outcomes end-to-endAttribution & trust → eval gates before promotion

    L2 is where the product is won or lost: the approval card is the highest-frequency object in the system, and every approve/deny is labeled training data for the improvement flywheel (monitor → analyze → plan → execute).

    Agent onboarding — hiring a digital employee

    Adding an agent to your fleet is an employment event, not an app install: the registry shows its eval score (reference check), the exact permission scopes it requests (least-privilege employment terms), and its autonomy ramp — every agent starts at L1 probation and earns promotion through your approvals. This is the "IT as HR department of digital employees" vision made into a screen — shown in the prototype's Agents tab.

    Metrics

    North star: hours returned per employee per week — cut by persona (focus hours for engineers, decision latency for execs). Funnel: digest open rate → ask/to-do → delegation conversion → approval latency (<2 min from push) → % completing unattended (L3 share). Counter-metrics: rollback rate, escalations, denials-without-reading (fatigue), RBAC violations (must be 0).

    Phased roadmap

    P1 Ask + triage digest (L0–L1) — earn the morning habit. P2 Plan preview + delegation + mobile approvals (L2) — earn trust. P3 Earned autonomy + open registry + role starter fleets (L3) — earn scale. P4 Fleet management & cross-agent workflows (L4 pockets) — the 100:1 company.
